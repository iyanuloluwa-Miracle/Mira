## Worry that gets harder to control

Some worry is a normal part of everyday life — before an exam, an interview, or a difficult
conversation. What Mira calls "symptoms of anxiety" is different: worry that shows up often,
feels hard to control, and starts to get in the way of daily life, sleep, or concentration.

## Common signs

People experiencing anxiety over a stretch of time often notice some combination of:

- Feeling nervous, on edge, or unable to relax, most days
- Worry that is hard to stop or control once it starts
- Worrying about many different things at once
- Trouble relaxing or sitting still
- Becoming easily irritated or annoyed
- Feeling a sense of dread, as if something bad is about to happen
- Physical signs — a racing heart, tense muscles, an upset stomach, trouble sleeping

The GAD-7 questionnaire you just completed asks about several of these directly, because they
tend to travel together.

## It is common, and it is treatable

Anxiety is one of the most common experiences people bring to a doctor or counsellor, anywhere
in the world. It is not a character flaw, and it does not mean something is "wrong" with you. It
usually responds well to support — from simple daily habits, to structured coping techniques, to
professional care when it is more persistent.

## What can help

- Slow, controlled breathing when anxiety spikes (see our grounding and breathing resource)
- Reducing caffeine, especially later in the day
- Regular sleep and movement, even in small amounts
- Naming the worry out loud to someone you trust, rather than carrying it alone
- Speaking with a licensed professional if anxiety is frequent or affecting your daily life

As with low mood, none of this replaces professional care where it is needed — it is a starting
point.
