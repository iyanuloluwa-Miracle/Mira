## Sleep and mood affect each other

Poor sleep can make low mood and anxiety worse, and low mood or anxiety can make it harder to
sleep well — it can become a cycle in either direction. Improving sleep will not fix everything
on its own, but it is often one of the more manageable places to start.

## Simple habits that tend to help

- **Keep a consistent wake-up time**, even on days off — this matters more than a fixed bedtime.
- **Get some daylight early in the day**, even a few minutes outside.
- **Cut back on caffeine after midday** — tea, coffee, and some soft drinks all count.
- **Wind down before bed** — dim lights, quieter activity, and putting your phone aside can help
  your body recognise that sleep is coming.
- **Keep your sleeping space for sleep** where you can — using the bed for work or long scrolling
  sessions can make it harder for your mind to settle there.
- **If you can't sleep after a while, get up briefly** rather than lying there frustrated, then
  try again when you feel sleepy.

## When sleep trouble needs more than habits

If poor sleep has lasted for weeks, is affecting your daily functioning, or comes with strong
low mood or anxiety, habits alone may not be enough — that is worth raising with a licensed
professional, who can look at what else might be going on.
