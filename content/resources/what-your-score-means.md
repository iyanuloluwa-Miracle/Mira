## You just answered two questionnaires

Mira asked you the PHQ-9 (nine questions about mood) and the GAD-7 (seven questions about
worry and anxiety). These are widely used screening questionnaires — they are a starting point
for a conversation, not an ending point.

## What a score is, and what it is not

Your score is a number that adds up how often you have noticed certain feelings over the last
two weeks. A higher number generally means those feelings have been showing up more often or
more strongly. That is genuinely useful information.

What it is not: a diagnosis. Only a qualified clinician, talking with you directly, can diagnose
a mental health condition. Two people with the same score can be going through very different
things, and the same person's score can change a lot from one week to the next.

## Why we show you a band, not just a number

Mira groups scores into bands — such as minimal, mild, moderate, or high — because a single
number ("I scored 11") is harder to make sense of on its own than a plain-language description
("your answers suggest mild symptoms"). The band is still just a summary of your answers, not a
judgment about you as a person.

## What happens next is up to you

A screening result is meant to help you decide what to do next, not to tell you what is wrong.
Depending on your result, that might mean reading more about what you are experiencing, trying
some general coping strategies, or talking to someone — a trusted person in your life, or a
licensed professional. All of those are reasonable next steps, and none of them require you to
have a diagnosis first.
