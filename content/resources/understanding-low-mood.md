## More than just sadness

Everyone feels sad sometimes — that is a normal part of life. What Mira calls "low mood" or
"symptoms of depression" is different: it tends to last longer, shows up most days, and can
affect your energy, your sleep, your appetite, your concentration, and how much you enjoy
things you used to enjoy.

## Common signs

People experiencing low mood over a stretch of time often notice some combination of:

- Feeling down, empty, or hopeless most of the day, nearly every day
- Losing interest or pleasure in activities that used to feel good
- Sleeping much more or much less than usual
- Feeling tired or low on energy, even after resting
- Trouble concentrating or making decisions
- Feeling slowed down, or restless and unable to settle
- Changes in appetite or weight without trying to change them

Having one or two of these once in a while is normal. Noticing several of them most days, for
two weeks or more, is worth paying attention to — which is exactly what a PHQ-9 screening like
the one you just completed is designed to help surface.

## It is common, and it is not a personal failing

Low mood affects people across every background, income level, and walk of life. It is not a
sign of weakness, laziness, or a lack of faith or willpower. It is also, for most people,
something that responds to support — whether that is rest, connection with others, changes to
daily routine, or professional care.

## What can help

- Talking to someone you trust about how you have been feeling
- Keeping some structure in your day, even small routines
- Gentle physical activity, even a short walk
- Reducing isolation — spending time with people, even briefly
- Speaking with a licensed professional, especially if low mood is affecting your daily life

None of this replaces professional care where it is needed — it is a starting point, not a
substitute for it.
