## You do not have to be an expert to help

If someone in your life is struggling with their mood or anxiety, you do not need to have all the
answers, or to fix things for them. Often, being present and taking them seriously matters more
than saying the perfect thing.

## Things that tend to help

- **Ask directly, and listen without rushing to solve it.** "How have you actually been doing
  lately?" and then giving them room to answer.
- **Take it seriously**, even if it does not make full sense to you or you would not react the
  same way.
- **Avoid minimising** — phrases like "just pray about it," "it's not that deep," or "other
  people have it worse" often shut the conversation down rather than helping.
- **Offer specific help**, not just "let me know if you need anything" — for example, offering
  to sit with them, go with them to an appointment, or check in again in a few days.
- **Look after yourself too.** Supporting someone else is easier to sustain if you are not
  running on empty yourself.

## If you are worried about their safety

If someone talks about wanting to harm themselves, take it seriously every time, even if it has
come up before. Stay with them if it is safe to do so, help them reach a trusted adult, health
facility, or emergency service, and see the "When to Seek Urgent Help" resource for more.

## What is not your job

You can support someone without being their therapist. Encouraging them toward a trusted person
or a licensed professional is not "giving up on them" — it is often the most helpful thing you
can do.
