## Small, doable steps

When mood is low or anxiety is high, even ordinary daily tasks can feel like a lot. This resource
is about small, realistic steps — not a demanding routine you have to follow perfectly.

## A loose daily structure

Structure does not have to mean a strict schedule. Even a rough shape to the day — a similar
wake-up time, a meal, some time outside, a wind-down before bed — gives your mind and body
something steady to rely on when everything else feels uncertain.

## Movement, in whatever amount is realistic

Movement does not need to mean exercise in the gym sense. A short walk, stretching, house chores,
dancing to a song you like — anything that gets your body moving a little counts, and it adds up.

## Eating and drinking

Skipping meals or relying heavily on caffeine and sugar can make low mood and anxiety feel worse.
You do not need a perfect diet — regular meals and enough water are a reasonable starting point.

## Connection, even in small amounts

Isolation tends to make things feel heavier. A short call, a text, or sitting with someone
without needing to talk about anything in particular can help, even when it feels like effort.

## Be realistic with yourself

The goal here is small, sustainable steps, not a complete transformation overnight. Doing one
small thing on a hard day is still doing something.
