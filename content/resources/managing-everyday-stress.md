## Stress is not the same as depression or anxiety

Some stress is a normal, even useful, part of life — it can sharpen focus before something
important. Problems tend to start when stress is constant, with no real break, or when it starts
to affect sleep, mood, relationships, or health. This resource is about the everyday kind, not a
substitute for the depression- and anxiety-focused resources elsewhere in this library.

## Common sources worth noticing

- Work, school, or financial pressure
- Family responsibilities and expectations
- Relationship difficulties
- Uncertainty about the future
- Simply having too much to do and too little time

Naming what is actually causing the stress — rather than just feeling generally overwhelmed — is
often the first useful step.

## Things that tend to help

- **Breaking large tasks into smaller ones.** A long list feels less overwhelming in pieces.
- **Protecting small pockets of rest**, even 10-15 minutes, rather than waiting for a full day
  off that may not come.
- **Movement** — a short walk, stretching, or anything that gets you physically moving.
- **Talking it through** with someone you trust, even just to say it out loud.
- **Noticing what you can and cannot control**, and putting your energy toward what you can.

## When stress becomes something more

If stress has been constant for weeks, is affecting your sleep or daily functioning, or comes
with persistent low mood or worry, it may be worth looking at the "Understanding Low Mood" or
"Understanding Anxiety" resources, or speaking with a licensed professional.
